In the North Atlantic, there's a remote island group called St Kilda.

It sits more than a hundred miles from mainland Scotland.

For up to nine months of the year, nobody could reach it.

So when the islanders needed food or medicine, they had only one way to call for help.

No radio. No telephone. No ship in sight.

They carved a tiny wooden boat with a hollow inside. They sealed a message in it, and tied it to a float made from a sheep's bladder.

Then they threw it into the sea, and hoped.

Nobody knew where it would land.

The ocean carried it for weeks, sometimes months. Some of those little boats reached the Scottish mainland. Others drifted all the way to Norway.

Then all they could do was wait.

Their only post office was the sea.
