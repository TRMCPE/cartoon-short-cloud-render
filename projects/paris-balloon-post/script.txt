In 1870, Paris was surrounded, and the only way out was up.

Enemy armies had closed every road, rail line, and telegraph wire.

So Parisians filled giant balloons with gas and sent their mail into the sky.

Dozens of balloons lifted off, often at night, drifting over enemy lines. Some carried passengers. Some carried millions of letters.

Even a government minister escaped that way, to raise a new army.

But the balloons couldn't fly back. They went wherever the wind took them.

So how did Paris get replies?

Pigeons. Each balloon carried birds out of the city. Then, once free, the pigeons flew home with messages.

Photographers shrank those messages onto tiny film, so one pigeon could carry thousands.

Enemy soldiers tried to shoot the balloons down, but many still got through.

A city with every road closed still found the sky.
