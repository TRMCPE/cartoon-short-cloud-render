On one island in the Pacific, the mailman had to swim.

Niuafo'ou is a volcano with no harbor, and the surf is too rough for ships to land.

So when a steamship appeared, the strongest swimmers paddled out, holding the island's letters up on long poles.

The crew lifted the letters aboard in buckets. Then they sealed the island's new mail inside a biscuit tin, and threw it into the sea.

The swimmers fought the waves, grabbed the floating tin, and pushed it all the way back to shore.

No dock. No boat. Just a tin can and a very good swimmer.

Letters sometimes took months.

Then the world noticed. Collectors everywhere wanted a letter stamped on that tiny island.

Soon it had a new name: Tin Can Island.

An island with no harbor became famous for its post office.
